# What a changed next action can tell us

**Exploratory (not preregistered)**

When an agent reads the database and then makes an implausible move, I want to know whether the read changed its decision at all. The transcript cannot answer that on its own. It contains the action taken after one particular observation. An alternative action might have been nearly as likely, or the same action might have appeared even if the observation had said the opposite. Those possibilities lead to different engineering questions.

One way to investigate is to replay the conversation prefix and score the legal action names. That gives a ranking under the supplied context. There is a catch: a legal action can take several tokens. A candidate can lead at the first token and lose when the score includes the rest of its name. A generated response can also continue past an otherwise legal name and get rejected. When I read a ranking, I want to see the tokenizer, the score convention, the terminal boundary and the actual decoded reply. Each is part of what the measurement means.

A second check is to replace an observed tool result while holding the surrounding history fixed. If the response changes, the replaced information had an effect in that constructed context. If it stays the same, the intervention may still have changed the relative scores of alternatives without crossing the decision threshold. A forced read narrows the question further. It lets us study the response to a result in episodes where the agent might never have requested that result for itself. A forced read should stay visible in the provenance of the comparison.

For Qwen, inspection’s mean opening probability within the scored legal menu changed from [0.0098](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-summary.json) to [0.2994](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-summary.json) with the reminder. The actual worker could still generate text outside that menu. The normalized figure is conditional on the comparison set. I would not use it as the probability that the live console will receive a valid inspection request. The first, superseded implementation produced a mean total variation of [0.0164](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-warm-controls.json) on [28](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-warm-controls.json) identical SmolLM2 inputs, larger than the apparent [0.0103](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-warm-controls.json) change when swapping the generic observation. With a comparison that moves even when the text stays fixed, I cannot interpret a small apparent effect of an altered observation. The study repeated the score measurements under cold contexts and checked repeats before interpreting changes. A separate unrestricted greedy replay reproduced the original reply, output count and stop reason on [268](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-audit.json)/[268](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-audit.json) Qwen decisions and [344](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-audit.json)/[344](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-audit.json) SmolLM2 decisions. That check helped distinguish the score implementation failure from a change to the frozen behavior. Canonical tokenization matched the naturally generated path for [266](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-audit.json)/[268](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/scores-audit.json) Qwen replies. A displayed word can have more than one legal token sequence. In a separate original-weight experiment, the unit steering intervention produced [0](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/whitebox/results.json)/[12](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/whitebox/results.json) exact submitted recoveries on Qwen and [0](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/whitebox/results.json)/[12](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-mechanism-v1.0.0/research/recovery/mechanism/whitebox/results.json) on SmolLM2. A change in an internal representation has to survive the action interface and the later turns before it can count as a recovered job.

A third kind of test uses internal activations. A classifier can sometimes predict a condition from those activations. Predictability alone does not show that the underlying model relied on that information to choose an action. The classifier might pick up a word that happens to occur only in one template. To interpret a readout responsibly, I want to know whether the train and test examples share a fixture, whether swapping the labels breaks the result, and whether a negative control succeeds for the wrong reason.

An intervention on those activations asks a more direct but still narrow question. Replacing a representation changes a computational state. A measured difference after replacement belongs to the chosen layer, position, readout and paired inputs. An effect on a scalar score is not automatically an effect on the tool call. An effect on the tool call is not automatically an improvement in the resulting database. Those distinctions matter most when several analysis choices were made after examining a failure.

The frozen benchmark gives these checks an anchor. Its [192](https://github.com/aravinds-kannappan/PodBench/blob/recovery-v1.0.0/docs/recovery-results.json) episodes have an immutable journal, verifiable tool semantics and a known budget. The new probes are follow-ups to what happened there. A convincing next test would make a prediction from one of them, commit it before seeing a different set of work orders, and then see whether the intervention changes both the predicted decision and the operational outcome. That is the point at which a descriptive pattern starts earning its keep as an explanation.
